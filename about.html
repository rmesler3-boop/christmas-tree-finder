How the heat map works

It is a likelihood ESTIMATE, not a survey of real trees. It ranks 30 m cells (LANDFIRE native resolution) by how likely they are to hold Christmas-tree-size (~6–12 ft) trees of the chosen species, using public datasets. Coverage: all 24 ranger districts of New Mexico's five national forests (Lincoln, Santa Fe, Carson, Cibola, Gila), each built separately and loaded when you zoom in (zoom ≥ 9). Not covered: Kiowa/Rita Blanca National Grasslands (no conifers) and BLM piñon/juniper permit areas (only PDF maps published). Levels are relative within each district (fifths of that district's scored, cuttable land), because forests differ a lot; “Best” means best in that district.
Colours: 5 levels relative to this area (each ≈ one fifth of the scored, cuttable land; very low scores are left transparent) – Low (blue, faint), Fair (teal), Good (yellow-green), Very good (orange), Best (magenta). Score = habitat match × weighted sum × elevation gate, inside the hard mask.
Habitat match (gate)LANDFIRE 2024 Existing Vegetation Type (e.g. “Madrean Upper Montane Conifer-Oak”, “Dry-Mesic Mixed Conifer” → fir 1.0)
Canopy height 20%LANDFIRE EVH: ≤5 m best, falls to 0.15 at 25 m (tall mature stands)
Canopy cover 12%LANDFIRE EVC: 25–60% best; dense >80% penalised
Regrowth 13%Inside MTBS burn (2000–2017), FACTS timber harvest or hazardous-fuel treatment completed 2000–2018 (≈9–26 yrs regrowth)
Elevation band 15%LANDFIRE/3DEP-derived elevation vs species band approx. bands, author estimate: South NM (Lincoln, Gila, Cibola Magdalena/Mountainair): fir 7,800–9,800 ft; spruce 9,500–11,800; ponderosa 6,800–8,800; pinyon 5,500–7,600. North NM (district centre ≥ 34.8° N: Santa Fe, Carson, Mt Taylor, Sandia): fir 8,000–10,200; spruce 9,500–12,000; ponderosa 7,000–9,000; pinyon 5,800–7,600. ±600 ft soft edge. All are estimates.
Aspect 10%LANDFIRE aspect: N/NE favoured for fir/spruce; S/SW mildly for pines
Slope 10%LANDFIRE 30 m slope (3DEP 10 m was tested on a 3×3 km Cloudcroft patch: mean slope 15.8° vs 14.6° – similar, so 30 m is kept to match the vegetation layers): ≤8° best, ≥35° ≈0 (carrying a tree out)
Wetness 5%Topographic Wetness Index computed from elevation (simple D8) – moist but not channel
Road access 15%Distance to MVUM roads open to vehicles: 60–400 m best; <30 m masked
No-cut mask (zero heat, light gray shading)Only land USFS owns (USFS Basic Ownership) and not BLM-SMA State/Private/Local/BIA land; minus wilderness, developed land (LANDFIRE), and setbacks from paved roads/highways, Forest roads, recreation sites, trails and perennial streams. Distances come from each forest's permit page or a labeled 150 ft DEFAULT (Rules tab; editable, applied live). Land status is a 30 m grid; road/trail/stream/rec-site setbacks are clipped from vector lines at render time.
All weights are tunable guesses. Your “🌲 Found good tree” spots (export from Saved) are the ground truth to recalibrate them.
Not integrated (yet): USDA SSURGO soils; USFS TreeMap/FIA plots; official designated cutting-area polygons (PDF maps only); closure-order polygons (PDF maps only – e.g. 2026 Agua Chiquita/Rio Peñasco/Wills Canyon order restricts camping & vehicles, Bluff Springs site closed – see fs.usda.gov/r03/lincoln/alerts); residences, power lines, plantations, active timber sales, lakes. No current wildfire perimeters in the area (NIFC WFIGS, checked Oct 10 2026).

Your field notes (local tuning)

Waypoints marked “Trees found: Yes/Some/No” adjust the heat map only on your phone, within 350 m of each mark, fading with distance: up to ×1.25 for Yes, ×1.10 for Some and ×0.65 for No. This is shown as gold (found) or gray dashed (not found) circles and affects “Best nearby” ranking. It is labelled “local tuning” and does not change the source data.

Base maps & their terms

  • USGS The National Map (Topo, Imagery+Topo): public domain – “Map services and data downloaded from The National Map are free and in the public domain.” (USGS FAQ). “Save area” offline downloads use these tiles only.
  • OpenStreetMap standard tiles: used for normal viewing only, with attribution and a normal browser Referer, per the OSM Tile Usage Policy, which forbids “Save area for later” / prefetch features. OSM tiles are never bulk-downloaded or stored offline by this app. © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
  • Esri World Street Map was removed: Esri requires an access token / ArcGIS account for basemap use, and that legacy service is retiring.

Lidar small-tree density (Sacramento RD test)

In a 5×5 km test box around Cloudcroft, a 1 m canopy height model was built from USGS 3DEP lidar (project NM_SouthEast_2018_D19, QL2, flown Nov 2018–May 2019, about 5 points/m² here). Small tree tops (local maxima 0.9–2.7 m tall in 2019) were counted, weighted toward open gaps, and assuming 0.5–1 ft/yr growth over 8 seasons (about +4 to +8 ft), so they are roughly Christmas size (5–13 ft) today. That density replaces most of the coarse LANDFIRE height factor (weight 0.35). West of about −105.47°, the Meta/WRI 1 m global canopy height map (2018 imagery) is used the same way at a lower weight (0.12), because it agreed poorly with lidar for small trees (r≈0.25 at 30 m). Both are ranked within their own source. These are estimates; small trees under tall canopy, and seedlings under ~3 ft in 2019, are missed.
